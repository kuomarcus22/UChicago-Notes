\styledchapter{Course Overview}
\vspace{-2\baselineskip}
The big question for this course is how to design economic institutions. These include:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Voting,
	\item Auctions,
	\item Matching, and
	\item Resource allocation.
\end{itemize}

Our role varies:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Sometimes we will be a benevolent planner (from outside the market).
	\item Other times, we will try to extract surplus.
\end{itemize}

Challenges include:
\begin{itemize}
	\item People are strategic (and reoptimize choices when the rules change).\footnote{Refer to the Boston school choice mechanism.}
	\item Information and power are decentralized.
\end{itemize}

We will formalize assumptions into mathematical axioms and use basic techniques like calculus, logic, linear programming, and linear algebra to answer our questions.

I refer to agents 1 and $i$ with female pronouns and agents 2 and $j$ with male pronouns.
